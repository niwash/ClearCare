## What is Fair Deal?

Fair Deal is the Irish government's scheme to help pay for long-term nursing home care. Its official name is the Nursing Homes Support Scheme (NHSS), and it has been running since 2009.

**You pay what you can reasonably afford, and the State (HSE) pays the rest.** Your contribution is worked out by looking at your income and your savings or property. If the nursing home costs more than your contribution, the HSE makes up the difference.

Fair Deal currently supports over 22,000 people in Ireland each year. It works for care in public nursing homes, private nursing homes, and voluntary (charitable) nursing homes.

---

## Who Can Apply?

Anyone who lives in Ireland and needs long-term nursing home care can apply. There is no minimum age, though the scheme is most commonly used by older adults.

You must meet two conditions:

1. **You must ordinarily live in Ireland** and have been living here for at least one year before applying.
2. **You must genuinely need full-time nursing home care.** This is checked through a care needs assessment (explained in the application steps below).

There is no income or savings threshold that stops you from applying. Whether you are well-off or have very little, you can use Fair Deal. The difference is how much you are asked to contribute.

---

## What Does Fair Deal Cover?

Fair Deal covers the main costs of being in an approved nursing home:

- Your room and bed
- All your meals
- Laundry
- The nursing and personal care you need

### What Fair Deal does NOT cover

Fair Deal does **not** pay for extras beyond standard care. You (or your family) will need to pay separately for things like:

- Hairdressing
- Therapies such as physiotherapy or occupational therapy (unless included in your care plan)
- Social activities or outings charged by the home
- Personal items such as clothing, toiletries, or newspapers
- Short-term care like respite stays or day care

Always ask the nursing home to give you a written list of what extra charges may apply. These will be set out in your contract with them.

## How Much Will You Pay?

The HSE works out how much you pay through a **financial assessment**. It looks at two things: your income and what you own.

### Your income

| Situation | Rate assessed |
|---|---|
| Single person | 80% of weekly income |
| Couple (combined income) | 40% of combined weekly income (per person in care) |
| Rental income from your family home (if rented while you are in care) | 40% of that rental income |

*For example: if you receive a State Pension of €265 per week, 80% of that is €212. That €212 goes toward your nursing home cost.*

### Your savings and assets

On top of income, 7.5% of the value of what you own is counted each year (3.75% for a couple's combined assets, split per person).

There is a **disregard**, a buffer amount that is not counted. Currently, the first €36,000 of assets for a single person (€72,000 for a couple) is not assessed at all.

### Your contribution is the same at every nursing home

Once the HSE calculates your weekly contribution, **that amount stays the same no matter which approved nursing home you choose.** If the home charges more than your contribution, the HSE pays the difference. If the home charges less, the State pays less, but you do not pay more than your assessed amount.

### Example 1: Single person

Mary is a widow. Her State Pension is €300 per week and she has €20,000 in savings (below the €36,000 disregard).

- Income contribution: 80% x €300 = **€240 per week**
- Asset contribution: €0 (savings are below the disregard)
- **Mary pays €240 per week.** If her nursing home costs €1,200 per week, the HSE pays the remaining €960.

### Example 2: Couple

Tom and Anne have a combined weekly income of €600 (two State Pensions). Tom moves into a nursing home. They have €50,000 in savings between them (below the €72,000 couple disregard).

- Income contribution: 40% x €600 = **€240 per week**
- Asset contribution: €0 (combined savings are below the disregard)
- **Tom pays €240 per week.** The HSE pays the rest of the nursing home cost above that.

Note: only 40% of the couple's combined income is used, not 80%, so Anne is not left without income while Tom is in care.

### Example 3: Renting out the family home

Brigid moves into a nursing home and decides to rent out her family home for €800 per month (about €185 per week). Her State Pension is €265 per week.

- Pension contribution: 80% x €265 = €212 per week
- Rental income contribution: 40% x €185 = €74 per week
- **Brigid's total weekly contribution: €286.**

The rental income is assessed at the lower rate of 40% (not 80%) because it comes from the family home. Any rent from a property that is not her main home would be assessed at the full 80%.

## The 3-Year Cap: Protecting Your Family Home

Many families worry that going into a nursing home means losing the family home. The 3-year cap is the rule that limits this.

### How it works

If you own your home, its value is included in the financial assessment. The HSE can count up to **7.5% of your home's value per year** as part of your contribution. For a home worth €300,000, that could be up to €22,500 per year (about €432 per week).

However, **after three years, the family home is no longer counted at all**, even if you remain in the nursing home for many more years. This three-year limit is called the **3-year cap**.

So the maximum the HSE can ever take based on the family home's value is 3 x 7.5% = **22.5% of its value.**

### The surviving spouse or partner is protected too

If your husband, wife, or civil partner is still living in the family home, a nursing home loan (explained in the next section) based on the family home will generally not be called in until that person has also passed away.

### What about the family farm or business?

Since 2021, the 3-year cap can also apply to a family farm or business, provided certain conditions are met, including that a family successor takes over and runs it for at least six years. A solicitor or the HSE can advise you on the specific conditions.

### If you sell your home while in care

The 3-year cap follows the money. If you sell your home while in a nursing home, the sale proceeds also qualify for the cap. They are only assessed for three years from when they were received.

## The Nursing Home Loan (Ancillary State Support)

Some people cannot cover their full contribution from income alone, particularly if most of what they own is their home. In that case, Fair Deal has an optional **nursing home loan**, officially called Ancillary State Support.

### What it is

Instead of paying the part of your contribution that relates to your home every week, the HSE covers it for you. That amount builds up as a loan, **tied to your home or other property**. You do not have to repay it while you are in the nursing home.

It is paid back later, usually after you pass away or when the property is sold.

### This is not a commercial bank loan

The loan is handled by Revenue (not a bank). There are no interest charges or fees under the standard scheme.

### Key things to know

- You apply for the nursing home loan at the same time as your Fair Deal application.
- Taking the loan is **completely optional**. You can instead pay the full amount from savings or income if you prefer.
- If your spouse or partner is still living in the home, the loan is generally not collected until they have also passed away.
- The loan is always capped by the 3-year rule. You will never be charged more than 22.5% of your home's value in total.

## How to Apply: Step by Step

Start the application as early as you can. **Funding cannot be backdated.** It is only paid from the date your application is approved, not from when you moved in.

1. **Get the application form.** Download it from [hse.ie/nhss](https://www.hse.ie/nhss/) or collect it from your local Community Care Office or Nursing Home Support Office.

2. **Submit your form** to your local Nursing Home Support Office. You can do this yourself, or a family member, solicitor, or social worker can help. If someone has Power of Attorney or is a court-appointed representative, they can apply on your behalf.

3. **Care needs assessment.** The HSE will arrange a visit to assess whether you need long-term nursing home care. This is usually done by a public health nurse or an HSE assessor.

4. **Financial assessment.** The HSE will look at your income and what you own to work out your weekly contribution. You will need to provide things like bank statements, details of any property you own, and your most recent tax notice from Revenue.

5. **Approval and nursing home selection.** If your application is approved, you will be told your weekly contribution. You can then choose from any approved nursing home (public, private, or voluntary). Your contribution amount stays the same regardless of which home you pick.

6. **Optional: apply for the nursing home loan.** If you want to use the loan to cover the part of your contribution linked to your home, apply for it at the same time as Step 2. It is not automatic; you have to request it.

> **Important:** If you move into a nursing home before your Fair Deal is approved, you or your family will need to cover the full cost in the meantime. Fair Deal will not cover any costs from before your approval date.

- Apply **as early as possible**. Funding only starts from the date of approval.
- You are free to choose **any approved nursing home**. Your contribution does not change based on which one you pick.
- The **family home is protected** after three years of the scheme.
- The **nursing home loan is optional** and is not a commercial bank loan.
- If you need help filling in the forms, Citizens Information, ALONE, or a solicitor can all assist you.
