# CLEARCARE — HIQA REPORT AUDIT
Findings, Patterns & Initial Gold-Set Notes — 16 Supplied Reports

JIRA: CCARE-13 — “Look into audit reports from HIQA and document the findings and patterns.”

Scope: all 16 HIQA PDFs supplied for this task, covering inspection reports from 2023 to 2026. This document is an audit of the supplied source material, not an independent assessment of the nursing homes.

## 1. What was audited

The 16 supplied PDFs were reviewed as a single audit batch. Fifteen are standard inspections and one — Navan Road Community Unit, 15 September 2023 — is a Restrictive Practice Thematic Inspection with a different structure. The thematic report is therefore retained as a separate report type and is not forced into the standard regulation schema.

The 15 standard reports contain 76 regulation-level judgements of “Substantially compliant” or “Not compliant” (50 Substantially compliant, 26 Not compliant). Each of these was cross-checked against the report's own Appendix 1 table of judgements, and the counts match for every report. Fourteen of the standard reports contain at least one such judgement; St John's House (06 July 2026) was judged Compliant on all 12 regulations assessed.

### 4.2 Main qualitative patterns

Premises and maintenance

Premises issues recur across the sample (Regulation 17 is the second most frequent judgement, 10 times across 9 centres). Findings include worn or damaged finishes, poor storage, damaged flooring, inadequate ventilation, damaged or inaccessible facilities, dirty windows and other environmental defects. Some are explicitly repeat findings from previous inspections (for example TLC Centre Santry 2023, Glengara Park 2023, Elmhurst 2026).

Fire safety

Fire precautions (Regulation 28) are the regulation most likely to be judged Not compliant (5 of 6 judgements), and fire-related findings also feature under Regulation 23. Examples in the supplied reports include escape-route obstructions, fire doors, emergency lighting, fire containment/fire-stopping, smoking arrangements, fire-drill records and external escape routes.

Infection prevention and control

Several reports link environmental cleanliness and maintenance to infection-control risk. Examples include visibly dirty equipment or surfaces, ineffective cleaning oversight, damaged surfaces that are difficult to clean, and inadequate cleaning records or systems. St Gabriel's 2026 notes the kitchen wash-up area was a repeat finding for the third inspection, and Croft 2025 records a repeat finding on a soiled toilet in use.

Governance and follow-through

Regulation 23 is the most frequent judgement (13 times, 6 of them Not compliant). A recurring distinction is between having a policy/audit system and demonstrating effective oversight. Reports identify cases where audits or previous compliance plans had identified issues but corrective actions, ownership, follow-up or monitoring were incomplete.

Staffing and training

Most staffing/training observations are positive, but partial-compliance findings identify issues such as staffing resources for housekeeping/maintenance, skill mix, supervision and gaps in mandatory training. Two reports (St Gabriel's 2026 and St Pappin's 2026) were judged Not compliant under Regulation 23 for staffing resources not matching the statement of purpose. The reports show why the system needs to retain the exact regulation judgement rather than infer a binary pass/fail.

Incident notification

Regulation 31 was judged Not compliant in all four reports where it appears (Hamilton Park 2023, Beechlawn House 2024, Croft 2025, Elmhurst 2026). The supplied reports include failures to notify certain incidents within required timeframes, including safeguarding incidents, peer-to-peer abuse, a hospital admission, and a notification wrongly included in the quarterly return.

Care plans and resident-specific documentation

Several reports identify gaps between assessment information and the current care plan, including outdated mobility information, incomplete risk information, or insufficient evidence of resident/family involvement. Other reports show strong person-centred care planning.

Residents’ rights, activities and complaints

Some reports identify insufficient access to meaningful activities, privacy/dignity concerns, complaints not fully resolved, or complaints procedures/information not being sufficiently accessible or prominent.

Repeat findings

The reports sometimes explicitly identify issues as repeat findings. This is particularly important for ClearCare because a useful comparison should show not only the current judgement but whether the same issue appeared in a previous inspection.

## 6. What this means for the ClearCare system

Each inspection should be stored as a dated event linked to a nursing-home entity, rather than treating the latest PDF as the home’s permanent status. St Pappin’s Nursing Home (OSV-0000178) appears twice in this batch and is a ready test case for this.

Each regulation judgement should retain three separate fields: regulation, exact HIQA judgement, and evidence/findings text.

Source page should be retained for every extracted finding so a user can verify the result against the original PDF.

Provider compliance-plan responses should be stored separately from inspector judgements. A provider saying it will fix an issue is not the same thing as the inspector judgement at the time of inspection.

Repeat findings should be linkable across inspections for the same centre and regulation.

Thematic inspections should have their own report type and schema because they do not follow the same regulation-by-regulation structure.

Extraction must not rely on the first “Regulation N: Title” line it finds. Cross-references inside narrative text repeat these headings, and a naive parser can attach a judgement to the wrong regulation. A reliable rule is to take only headings that match the report’s Appendix 1 list, and to reconcile the extracted judgements against the Appendix 1 table.

When extraction confidence is low, the system should flag the item for review rather than inventing a regulation/judgement association.

## 7. Thematic inspection — Navan Road Community Unit

The 15 September 2023 Navan Road report is a Restrictive Practice Thematic Inspection. Its stated purpose is quality improvement around restrictive practices. It discusses restrictive practices from qualitative and quantitative perspectives and describes governance measures such as a restrictive-practice committee, audits, a restraint register, staff training, consent/documentation and review of less restrictive alternatives.

The report records an overall Compliant judgement. Because this report does not follow the same regulation-level judgement structure as the standard inspections, it should be stored as a thematic inspection in ClearCare rather than treated as missing regulation data.

## 8. Limitations and audit notes

This document uses only the 16 PDFs supplied for the task. It does not claim to represent all HIQA nursing-home inspections.

The reports span 2023–2026 and cover different centres, so the recurring themes are descriptive patterns in this batch, not sector-wide prevalence estimates.

The reports differ in structure and in the number of regulations discussed (from 8 to 16 per report), so regulation counts are not a like-for-like measure across centres.

Compliance plans and provider responses attached to the reports are not summarised here; only inspector judgements and findings are recorded.

No independent judgement has been made about whether a nursing home is “good” or “bad”. The document reports HIQA’s own findings and judgements.
