# A job is due by the day, and new reports are looked for every day

A job of the pipeline's schedule that waits a day or more is due on the UTC day its wait ends, whatever the time, and the reports job waits one day after a success, as the register job does. The wait is counted from the job's last success with the wait the code has now, so the state file's own next attempt decides only after a failed or unfinished download. The alerts dispatcher, which waits an hour, is still due by the minute. A daily look for reports asks HIQA for each centre's page, about 550 requests at least 3 seconds apart, which takes about half an hour, and downloads only the report files not stored yet.

ADR-0014 had a job due at the time of its last start plus its wait, and the reports downloaded once a week. On AWS the task starts at 06:00 UTC every day and a job starts some minutes into it, so a run that reached a job earlier than the run before found it not due and left it for the next day. On 20 November 2026 the weekly look was missed by five minutes, and reports HIQA published on 13 November reached the site on 21 November. Allowing some minutes of slack would have hidden the gap only until a run was faster by more than the slack. Starting each job at a fixed time of its own would need a scheduled task per job, when one task a day runs them in order under one lock (ADR-0022). A weekly look that never slipped would still show a report up to a week late.

From now on a report HIQA publishes is on the site after the next morning's run. A long-running pipeline (Compose) runs its daily jobs in the first hourly check after midnight UTC, not 24 hours after the last start, and a failed download is tried again the next day, which near midnight is less than a day later. If HIQA asks for fewer requests, lengthen the reports job's wait; the stored state follows it at the next run.
