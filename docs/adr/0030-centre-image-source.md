
# A centre's photo comes from a Google place identifier we store

`clearcare.centre_image` holds one row for each centre whose photo we can show: the identifier of the Google listing matched to it, and when it was matched. A centre with no row has no acceptable listing, and the page shows our placeholder. It holds nothing else: no coordinates, no photo URL, no copy of a photo. The page renders the photo from the identifier in the visitor's browser, through Google's Places UI Kit, so no image passes through our servers and none is stored. That request goes from the visitor's browser to Google, which therefore sees their IP address; the privacy notice says so (ADR-0028). For that reason nothing is requested on page load: the frame shows a short note and a "Show photos" button, and Google's library is not loaded until it is pressed. The agreement is kept for the visit in `sessionStorage`, so a family reading several homes is asked once.

An identifier is what makes this possible under Google's terms. The EEA Service Specific Terms let the coordinates of a place be cached for at most 30 consecutive calendar days (ADR-0027), but they place no such limit on a place identifier, which may be kept indefinitely. That is why `centre_image` has no expiry column and no job deletes its rows, unlike `clearcare.centre_location`. Storing the identifier and re-fetching the photo on every view is the only arrangement that keeps the photo current and stays inside the terms; caching the image, or the coordinates behind it, would not.

A listing is approved only when four conditions hold: Google returned a listing for the centre; the register name and Google's name agree, either by spelling similarity of at least 0.70 or by overlap of the meaningful words of at least 0.60; the listing lies within 300 m of the point in `centre_location`; and the listing has at least one photo. A centre that fails any of them gets no row, and the page shows our own placeholder.

A row therefore means one thing: we have an image for this home. There is no rejected state, because a refusal has nothing to display and the page falls back to the placeholder either way.

The stage asks about a centre when its point was geocoded on the day of the run, and never when the centre has no point at all: there is nothing to measure a candidate against, so the two centres without an Eircode are never asked and keep the placeholder. The freshness condition is what makes the daily run cheap and what keeps a stored identifier honest. The geocode stage re-fetches every point 23 days old or more (ADR-0027), so on that day the whole register holds a point from today and every centre is matched again; on the days between, only a centre appearing in the register for the first time is. A home that fails the rule therefore costs one request every 23 days rather than one a night, and a home that moves is matched again against its new point, which is also the only way a stored identifier is replaced. A re-match that fails leaves the row it had: one failed search is not evidence that the image is wrong, and a home that has moved keeps its old photo until a re-match lands or a person deletes the row. The stage's `--all` option asks about every centre whose point is still inside Google's thirty days, for the first run and for a re-match after the rule changes.


The listing is found by searching for the centre's name and address, not by looking for businesses near its point. Both were run across the register. The name search approves 324 centres; searching the businesses within 300 m of the point approves 319. The radius search cannot return a namesake in another town, but it offers wrong-but-nearby businesses that a name query never ranks first: its six extra homes included a marina, a neighbouring nursing unit and the hospital beside a unit. The name search was kept, because it approves more homes and the distance check below catches the far namesakes it can return.

Neither method is exact, and the size of the error is not known. A spot check of the thirty-one weakest name matches among the 324 found one probable wrong building: a health centre on the campus of the community hospital it was matched to. The other thirty were the same home under a different name, an operator's name, or a spelling variant - Millstreet Community Hospital for St Joseph's Community Hospital, for instance, which is one building and not an error. Those cases pass because the only distinctive word left after the generic ones are removed is the town's name, and no comparison of names alone will separate two facilities on one campus. Because the check covered only the weakest names it is a floor and not a rate, so the approved list is reviewed by a person once before launch, and the homes with no identifier keep the placeholder.

`place_id` is not unique. Two centres in one campus can resolve to the same Google listing, and a constraint would fail a load for a correct match.

The API reads these rows through `clearcare.centre_listing`, a view granted to `clearcare_api` (db/V20), as it does for the points (ADR-0026); V19 itself grants nothing to that role. The centre's record carries the identifier as `place_id`, and the page renders the photo from it in the visitor's browser. Which homes display a photo, and the cost of doing so, is CCARE-209's proposal; this record covers only what is stored and for how long.
